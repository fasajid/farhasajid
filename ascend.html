The short version

The problem

First-generation students need clear, accessible guidance to make informed decisions and finish each step of the college process with confidence.

My role

Research, UX strategy, information architecture, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, visual design and accessibility.

The approach

A guided experience that shows students what matters to them and what to do next, instead of more information.

The outcome

Task completion rose from 60% to 100% across core flows after two rounds of testing.

Chapter 1 · Research

Mixing the paste

Understanding the students before designing anything.

Background

College information is scattered across college websites, FAFSA, scholarship pages, application portals and deadlines. Ascend brings exploration, financial aid, deadlines and step-by-step roadmaps into one place.

How I researched

I interviewed five first-generation high school seniors from immigrant families about applications, financial aid, family expectations and how prepared they felt. Then I grouped what I heard with affinity mapping, and compared four major college-planning and financial-aid platforms.

  • Financial uncertainty
  • Guidance & preparation
  • Emotional pressure
  • Timing & responsibilities

The assumption I got wrong

I expected a lack of family support to be the biggest barrier. It wasn't. Families were supportive, but often unfamiliar with the U.S. college system. That changed the question I was designing for:

How can I give students more college information?

→

How can I help students understand what matters to them, and know what to do next?

Competitive analysis

Meet Sara

Sara is the first in her family to apply to college. She represents the goals, needs and circumstances of the students I interviewed.

Chapter 2 · Ideation & design

Applying the design

From paper sketches to a clear, guided flow.

From sketch to prototype

I started with paper sketches of personalization and homepage layouts, moved to digital wireframes, then connected a low-fidelity prototype to test the full journey from onboarding to action.

Three core experiences

  1. College search & save

    Explore colleges that fit and keep track of favorites.

  2. Financial aid & opportunities

    FAFSA, scholarships and aid explained in plain language.

  3. Roadmap & progress

    Step-by-step milestones, so students always know what's next.

Design system

Logo & color

An upward mark signals progress. Deep navy builds trust, while orange, yellow, cream and light blue add warmth and highlight actions and states.

Typography

Charis SIL for brand personality, Roboto for readable interface text.

Navigation

Four tabs: Home, Colleges, Financial Aid and Profile.

Feedback

Checkmarks, completion indicators, selected states and progress trackers, so students can see how far they've come.

Designed for every student

  • AaClear visual hierarchy, so the most important task stands out.
  • ◐Meaning never relies on color alone: icons, labels and text carry it too.
  • 👆Large touch targets with clear spacing.

Chapter 3 · Test & outcome

Letting the stain develop

Two rounds of testing, and the changes that came from them.

Testing & iterations

Two rounds of usability testing with five participants covered onboarding, homepage navigation, saving a college, exploring aid and finding next steps.

AreaWhat I foundWhat I changed
Homepage2 of 5 found it overwhelmingSimplified the layout and strengthened hierarchy for priority tasks
Roadmap4 of 5 couldn't tell completed, current and upcoming milestones apartRedesigned progress states and milestone indicators
Financial aid3 of 5 needed more context on FAFSA requirementsAdded explanations and clearer links between learning and action
InteractionsButtons and icons didn't show where they ledRefined labels, button hierarchy and interaction cues

60% → 100%task completion across core flows

Reflection

What I learned

The best experience gives the right information at the right moment, not more information.

Design around what users actually need instead of adding features, and challenge your own assumptions. Mine about family support turned out to be wrong, and the product got better because of it.

Next steps: test with a broader group of first-generation students, add college comparisons and personalized aid guidance, and keep testing accessibility.
